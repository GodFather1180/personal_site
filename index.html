Detecting shrinkflation in Canadian grocery prices

Do products get smaller while their price per unit goes up? Using ~70 million scraped price observations from 8 Canadian grocery chains, I built a pipeline that parses package sizes out of free text, matches relaunched products across listings with TF-IDF, and tests the price effect against a control group. I then benchmarked a price index built from the same data against Statistics Canada's Food CPI.

  • 2,214 shrinkflation candidates found. Median price-per-unit change was +19.0% versus +0.3% for 49,209 same-size control pairs (Mann–Whitney U, p < 0.001).
  • The effect differs by chain (Kruskal–Wallis, p ≈ 4×10⁻⁷), from +31.5% at Save-On-Foods to about +13% at No Frills and Galleria.
  • Found and corrected 100× pricing errors in 42,578 rows; the pipeline is covered by 78 automated tests.
  • Switching the index from an arithmetic to a geometric (Jevons) mean moved its growth from +13.7% to +1.8%, against +6.5% for official CPI.

What makes a Reddit post succeed, and do city subreddits scale with population?

A two-part analysis of 2022–2023 Reddit data on the SFU Hadoop cluster. Part one models what makes a post succeed from timing and title features. Part two joins subreddit activity to 2021 Census populations and fits a power law to see which Canadian cities are busier or quieter than their size predicts.

  • PySpark pipeline on HDFS that reduced 18M Reddit records (150 subreddits) to 2M observations; an explicit schema and a narrow read path brought extraction down to about 37 minutes.
  • Scores differ by subreddit (Kruskal–Wallis) and weekday comments run higher (Mann–Whitney U), both p < 0.001.
  • Gradient-boosted classifier for above-median engagement: 82% accuracy, 0.80 F1, 0.87 AUC. Top predictors were subreddit size, posting time, title length and early engagement.
  • Power-law fit of regional subreddit activity against population: exponent ≈ 0.85, R² ≈ 0.78.
